## Findings
1. **Every loss is site-wide, not page-specific** (`a10.py`). In each period the pages with ≥ 80 visits/week lose about the same share whatever their type or age. Example A (Aug → Nov 2023): middle half of pages kept 47–72 %; reviews under 3 months old kept 77 %, 3 years+ 53 %. Example C1 (week of 6 May 2024): reviews 0.87, old reviews 0.87, comparisons 0.86; every age group 0.84–0.91. Exceptions: guides in Aug–Sep 2023 (kept 48 % vs 83 % site median), articles in Oct 2023 (kept 56 %).
2. **In 2023 the two core updates moved more than the helpful content update.** Per-page median kept: mid-Aug → mid-Sep (Aug core, 22 Aug–7 Sep) 0.83; 11 Sep → 2 Oct (HCU, 14–28 Sep) 0.94; 2 Oct → end Oct (Oct core, 5–19 Oct) 0.74. No year-before data, so the seasonal part of Aug → Oct is unknown; US −37 % vs AU −19 % (spring there) says most of it is not season.
3. **Three steps start days before an announced update**, and trackers reported unconfirmed volatility on the same dates: 7–10 May 2024 (reports for 3 and 9 May), 2 Dec 2025 (reports 3–4 Dec; core announced 11 Dec), 19–20 May 2026 (seoClarity: shifts a week before the 21 May announcement).
4. **2 Dec 2025 in Search Console (API, web search, 17–23 Nov vs 3–9 Dec):** impressions 2.40M → 1.76M (−27 %), clicks 33.8k → 23.6k (−30 %); product-snippet results 806k → 500k impressions; desktop impressions −41 %, mobile −12 %; "[shoe] review" queries lose 60–90 % of impressions at the same average position (e.g. "novablast 5 review" 590 → 102 a day, position 4.8 → 5.0). Discover clicks 744 → 28 a week.
5. **Since Aug 2025 comparisons lose clicks at an unchanged rank** (`g2.py`, mobile, queries containing " vs "): Aug 2025 5.4k impressions/day, position 3.9, CTR 14.8 %, 803 clicks/day → Oct 2026 5.8k impressions/day, position 4.4, CTR 3.2 %, 186 clicks/day. "review" queries: impressions 9.2k → 5.2k/day, position 5.4 → 6.7, CTR 9.6 → 3.8 %, clicks 891 → 196/day.
6. **Discover is not hidden inside the totals.** New-review spikes do arrive as "Organic Search" in GA4 (Infinity RN4: 2,836 visits on day 2), but spikes above 1.5× a page's running median are ≤ 4 % of weekly visits in any month (`a9.py`).
