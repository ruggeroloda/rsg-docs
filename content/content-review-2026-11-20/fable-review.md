# RSG content review

Nov 20, 2026 · @Ruggero

## Summary

1. Search clicks fell 68% (187k to 60k) and were still falling 14% from September to October 2026. No floor is visible yet.
2. Two things drive most of the loss: far fewer new reviews (75 published in 2025, 8 in 2026) and a 50–60% lower yield per page at the same age. CTR is the smaller part.
3. Comparisons are the engine: 39% of clicks, 2.3× the review conversion rate, and no shoe purchase needed. New-vs-previous-version pages convert best.
4. The fixable on-page problems are stale dates in titles, dead-end comparisons, purchase-intent pages with zero affiliate events, and an ad covering the score and verdict on the first phone screen.
5. 2–3× traffic is not reachable through Google at 1 shoe a month. My estimate is about 40k search clicks a month in a year, give or take 10k.
6. Getting there needs roughly 3× the shoe coverage or a non-search channel, and the pack cannot size either.

Facts are from the fact pack of 20 Nov 2026. Anything marked (H) is my hypothesis.

## 1. What works and what doesn't

### Facts

- **Output collapsed.** Reviews with any click, by publication year: 145 (2023), 107 (2024), 75 (2025), 8 (2026).
- **Fresh pages still earn.** The 8 reviews from 2026 earned 837 clicks each in the 90-day window, against 966 each for 2025 reviews in the 2025 window. The 8 are the biggest shoes, so the true like-for-like drop is larger than 13%.
- **Older pages decay faster than before.** One-year-old reviews earned 757 clicks per page in 2025 and 297 in 2026 (−61%). Two-year-old reviews went from 329 to 158 (−52%).
- **Comparisons are cheaper and convert better.** New 2026 comparisons earn 782 clicks per page, close to a new review, at 2.77 key events per 100 views against 1.20 for reviews.
- **Version-vs-predecessor converts best.** Ghost 17 vs 16 is at 9.36 per 100, Novablast 5 vs 4 at 7.34, Bondi 9 vs 8 at 5.24, Endorphin Speed 5 vs 4 at 4.86. These pages lose search clicks fastest once a newer version exists (Nimbus 27 vs 26: −86%).
- **ASICS is the franchise.** 18 of the top 40 pages are ASICS, and they convert (Superblast vs Novablast 4.62, Novablast 5 review 3.49).
- **Nike pages convert poorly.** Pegasus 42 review 0.61, Vaporfly vs Alphafly 0.66, Zoom Fly vs Vaporfly 0.51, Best Nike list 0.94.
- **Old reviews are the most resilient in search** (−50%, CTR flat at about 0.9%) but the weakest commercially: 1.77% of visits click a shop, against 5.08% for current reviews.
- **Best-of lists convert as well as comparisons** (2.79 per 100) but get 2.4% of views.
- **Guides proper are negligible.** /guide/ pages get 88–133 clicks a month, and no guide sample is in the pack, so I treat the best-of page as the guide.

### Headroom

- Queries at position 4–15 with at least 1,500 impressions total about 2.1M impressions and 9.6k clicks per 90 days. Doubling CTR there adds roughly 3–5k clicks a month, under 10% of traffic.
- 860k comparison impressions sit at position 6–10 with 0.52% CTR, against 5–6% at positions 1–2. This is the largest pool, but the pack shows no lever that moves position.
- Your own site search asks for 8 unreviewed shoes. Three of them (Novablast 6, Superblast 3, Kayano 33) are ASICS.

## 2. Where the click is lost

### Facts

- **Review loss is mostly impressions.** Reviews: implied impressions −63%, CTR −32% (August 2025 to October 2026). Comparisons: −52% and −33%.
- **"Review" queries kept their CTR.** It rose from 1.81% to 2.11% at the same position, but impressions fell 75% (5.8M to 1.4M).
- **"Versus" queries are the clean SERP-side case.** Impressions rose 22% at the same position (4.8 to 5.0) while CTR fell from 9.05% to 2.72%. No title or date explains a 70% drop across a whole class.
- **The top positions lost most.** Review CTR at position 1 fell from 3.84% to 1.09%, and positions 1–2 now sit below positions 4–5 (2.09%).
- **Broad queries gained position without clicks.** Best/top went from 25.3 to 8.9, the homepage from 29.6 to 8.4, and alternatives from 7.1 to 4.1 with CTR falling from 3.65% to 0.17%.
- **The CTR recovery since April is a shrinking denominator.** Comparison CTR rose from 1.11% to 1.85%, but implied impressions fell from 4.6M to 1.3M and clicks from 51k to 23k.

### Hypotheses

- Many 2026 position 1–3 impressions are probably links inside AI or other SERP blocks, which are counted as high positions and rarely clicked. That would explain the inverted CTR curve and the spring impression bulge.
- The 75% fall in "review" impressions is some mix of fewer searches, fewer ranked queries (lost rankings vanish from Search Console) and fewer fresh pages. The pack cannot split it.

### AI Overviews versus the page

Not enough data for a percentage. The versus loss is predominantly SERP-side. Between a fifth and just under half of the review loss is from publishing 8 instead of 75 (my estimate from the per-page yields in section 1).

### Fixable on your side

- Dates in titles and bylines.
- Pages that end without a next step.
- Monetisation gaps on pages with buying intent.
- Coverage of shoes people already search for on your site.

## 3. The four pages

I can't see the live SERP, so this reads the pages against what a summary could already give.

### Pegasus 42 review (0.61 per 100)

- **First screen:** the floating video ad covers the score and half the verdict, the two things the searcher came for. Fixed bars take about a fifth of the screen.
- **Sticky price:** "$77.90 at Zappos" is the women's price, unlabelled. Men's is $87.
- **Sticky bar clicks:** 5 of 394 shop clicks in three days. It may only have been live since 18 Nov.
- **No path for the "skip it" reader.** Brandon names better options (Vomero Plus, Ride 19, Cumulus 28, Clifton 10, Ghost 17), but the compare table shows the Nimbus 28 and Vomero 18. A 7/10 review needs a "buy this instead" exit.
- **Unique material is buried.** The run log (about 82 km), "owned 10 versions", and "if you have the 41, skip the 42" sit below eight paragraphs of Pegasus history that a summary reproduces.
- **Small contradiction:** the verdict says "up to 21 km" while the log has a 23 km run.

### Novablast 5 review (3.49 per 100, clicks −87%)

- **Frozen in January 2025.** It compares against the Ghost 16, Ride 17, Clifton 9 and Novablast 4, with a Nimbus 26 in the table.
- **No mention of the Novablast 6**, which your visitors search for.
- **Price mismatch:** the text says $140, the price box says list $150.
- **Wrong angle for today.** It is now the discounted previous model. That is why it converts, and the page doesn't say so.
- **Minor errors:** "stability of versions 4 & 5" in the conclusion, stack shown as 41 mm and 41.5 mm.

### Ghost vs Adrenaline (4.98 per 100)

- **Works:** verdict, Buy-if, gendered prices and a one-variable decision (support).
- **Second-hand voice.** It is written by the publisher quoting "our tester", which is a synthesis of two reviews. An AI answer does the same synthesis (H: this is why versus CTR fell).
- **Dead end.** No link to Ghost vs Glycerin or Ghost 17 vs 16. Comparisons run at 1.29 pages per visit, and only 14.1% of visits see a second page.
- **Redundant rows.** The table lists identical values (brand, drop, best for).
- **Title and date.** The H1 has no version numbers, and "Published 19 Feb 2020" is displayed.

### Best Running Shoes Right Now (4.42 per 100)

- **Broken promise.** The intro promises "what else we considered", but each category has one pick and no runner-up.
- **Score credibility.** The Winflo 11 is 10/10 beside the Novablast 5 at 9/10.
- **Stale signals.** "One of the best carbon racers of 2025", "Hoka One One", and eight sibling lists titled "(August 2024)" or "(October 2024)".
- **Generic filler.** The how-to-pick and FAQ sections are what a summary answers in full. One category intro is a single sentence.
- **Don't chase the 317k impressions.** They are probably not real demand (H).

## 4. Structures

### Review

1. Title with the verdict and distance tested, for example "7/10 after 82 km".
2. Score and one-line verdict, with nothing covering them.
3. Three exits: this shoe (gender-labelled price), the previous version if cheaper, and the tester's named alternative.
4. Presenter verdict, 60–90 seconds (H: no video data in the pack).
5. Pros and cons, fit, facts.
6. Test log and "vs the last version", with b-roll clips in the upper and sole sections.
7. Compare table against the shoes the tester names, linked to comparisons.
8. Prices.
9. Next step: three comparisons and a price-drop alert sign-up.

### Comparison

1. Title with version numbers.
2. Verdict and Buy-if.
3. Prices, with the cheaper shoe flagged.
4. Both shoes' b-roll cut together with presenter voice-over. This supplies first-hand evidence without a new shoot.
5. Differences only.
6. "If you run in X now" switching advice.
7. Three neighbouring comparisons and the Shoe Finder.

### Guide or best-of

1. A current month in the title, only when actually updated.
2. Picks grid.
3. Per category: pick, runner-up, "skip if", and a link to the pick-vs-runner-up comparison.
4. Presenter lineup video per brand (H).
5. Shoe Finder in place of the generic how-to-pick text. It holds 50 seconds of engagement at 2.71 per 100 on 1% of views.
6. A "tell me when the picks change" sign-up.

## 5. Allocation and the 2–3× question

### Monthly mix

- 1 flagship review.
- 2–3 comparisons spawned from it: always vs the predecessor, plus a sibling and a cross-brand rival.
- 5–6 comparison refreshes, until the remaining 34 outdated ones are done.
- 1 best-of refresh.
- 1 brand lineup guide per quarter, ASICS first. The Puma lineup piece is the only article that grew (192 to 3,235 views), but that is one page.
- No news posts.

### Why 2–3× doesn't work this way

- New content adds about 11k clicks a month by the end of year one: 12 reviews at roughly 280 a month each, plus 30 comparisons at roughly 260.
- The existing base (60k) has been losing 50–70% a year.
- Net: about 40k a month, down from 60k.
- Depth cannot close the gap. The site runs at about 1.9 pages per visit, and doubling page views at flat visits needs 3.8.

### What would have to change

- **Coverage of 30–40 shoes a year**, using samples for secondary coverage. That means rewording the "No free samples" box, which currently appears on every page.
- **A channel Google doesn't control.** Email brought 13 visits in three days from a 50k list. YouTube is unsized.
- **Or a different goal:** revenue per visit. Shop clicks per page view look about 50% higher on the new design than the 2026 average, but that compares two tools over three days in a pre-Black-Friday week, so treat it as suggestive.

### Your bets

- **"Google will send more clicks to links again."** The pack points the other way so far: clicks fell every month since March 2026.
- **"AI citations will matter."** AI referrals were 2 of about 8,500 visits. The traffic value today is nil, and brand payments can't be assessed here.
- **"Fewer ads convert better."** Untested. The first-screen overlap is the place to test it.
