# What an RSG review should be: an independent view (22 Nov 2026)

_Answer to `BRIEF-REVIEW-FORMAT-2026-11-22.md`, written by a Fable chat that had not seen the earlier chat's opinions. **Nothing is decided and nothing was built.** Ruggero decides at "Decisions for Ruggero" (bottom). Data: `review-format-2026-11-22/reviews-2026-11-22.csv` (367 scored reviews; the numbers below are road, 303 reviews, unless said). Competitor pages read live on 22 Nov 2026._

## The short answer
1. **Text (A): yes to Ruggero's idea 3.** A new review is organised around what is rated, with two conditions: the rated parts are **four**, not ten, and the tester's testing gets its own short block before them. It is not a risk: the strongest sites already write this way.
2. **Scoring (B): four parts: Ride, Fit & comfort, Build & durability, Value.** New reviews are scored on the four; the 367 old ones are *shown* in the same four, worked out from their ten stored scores. Nothing is rewritten, old and new stay comparable.
3. **"The parts say X, we say Y": no.** The data says the gap is mostly the tester's scoring habit, not a personal reason. Replace it with one sentence, **"Why 7?"**, written by the tester.
4. **"Versus the average shoe in the category": no.** The category averages are all the same number (7.9), and the tester matters more than the category. Compare with **named shoes, in words** (last year's version, one or two rivals).
5. **Categories:** no new type is needed for any of this. Park it.
6. **What this will and won't do:** it makes the new tester's brief clear, puts the best material (how it rides) first in the text, and gives every review hooks to its comparisons. There is no evidence anywhere that a review format wins traffic back (`STRATEGY.md`, "Peers"). Publishing is still the lever.

## What the 367 reviews say (my own reading of the CSV)
- **The ten sub-scores hold about three pieces of information.** The four ride scores (sole unit, landing, transition, toe-off) are one score given four times: all four identical in 122 of 303 reviews, within 1 point in 250. Four parts (ride, fit & comfort, build & durability, value) explain the expert score exactly as well as all ten (71 % of its variation either way).
- **The scale is used as a 4-point scale.** 68 % of all sub-scores are 8 or higher, 12 % are 10/10, 3 % are 5 or lower.
- **The tester matters more than the shoe type.** Average expert score per tester (10 testers with 8+ road reviews): from 7.47 to 8.44. Average per "Best for" group: daily training 7.93, speedwork 7.92, racing 7.90, long distance racing 8.22. A mark for "the category average" would sit at the same place on every review; what moves is who scored.
- **The gap between the expert score and the average of the parts is a habit.** Brandon Law (131 reviews): average gap −0.01, 0.5 or more apart in 19 reviews. Larry Lam (21 reviews): expert score lower than his own parts by 0.5+ in 14, and 40 % of all his sub-scores are 10/10. Showing "parts say 8.8, we say 7" on those pages tells the reader the parts are inflated, not that the tester had a personal reason.
- **Versus last year's model, in numbers, is also weak.** 126 road reviews could be matched to the review of their predecessor: score unchanged in 44, lower in 44, higher in 38; **only 53 of the 126 were scored by the same tester.** As a number it mostly compares testers. As text ("smoother than the 41, same foam, no need to upgrade") it is what the reader asks.
- **Durability and traction scores say almost nothing** about the verdict (correlation with the expert score 0.26 and 0.19), and durability is scored after about 80 km (Pegasus 42: 7 runs, 81.6 km).
- **Live check, Pegasus 42:** nothing in the text explains any of the ten numbers. Ride is 6/6/6/6, the upper 9, the expert score 7; the reader has to work out alone that the ride decided it. **Cascadia 19 (trail):** nine 10s and an expert 10/10; the text sections (protection, durability, responsiveness, comfort and fit) are already "one section per aspect", but none is tied to a score.

**Common:** nobody scores ten things (three or four parts, or scores per use); where parts are scored, the text sections are those parts (Running Warehouse, RunRepeat, RTINGS, roughly Road Trail Run); every one compares with **named shoes**; "who is it for" sits high.
**Distinctive:** RunRepeat and RTINGS own "measured versus the average" because they measure with machines, one method, hundreds of shoes. Believe in the Run owns personality. Solereview answers first.
**RSG should copy:** few parts that are also the sections; a paragraph per named rival; the answer and "who for" high (already there).
**RSG should avoid:** "versus average" without a lab (opinion scores from 17 testers are not measurements); scores per use (the old reviews have none and they cannot be worked out afterwards); two testers per shoe (budget is one shoe a month).
**RSG can own:** one named runner who **bought the shoe** and shows the test: how many km, how many runs, the longest, the paces. Believe in the Run gives almost none of that; the lab sites have no runner. Brandon's reviews already contain it, buried in the introduction.

## A. Text format for new reviews (question 1)
Total text about 1,300 words, as today (median 1,359). The page is not shortened; the order changes.

| # | Section | Its job | Length | Comes from today's |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| top | Summary, score, **"Why 7?"** line, verdict, pros/cons, price, who should / should not buy | The answer | as now + 1 sentence | same |
| 1 | **How I tested it** | The personal part: who I am as a runner (weight, size, foot strike), bought where, km, number of runs, longest run, paces, surfaces, weather; 2–3 sentences of first impression. **First person.** The numbers also go in fields so the page can show them as a strip | 80–120 words | Introduction (test list) + First impressions |
| 2 | **Ride** | How it feels from landing to toe-off, at which paces it works, how far, stability. Ends with one paragraph "vs the [previous version]" | 350–400 | Sole unit |
| 3 | **Fit & comfort** | Sizing, toe box, lockdown, heat, comfort over the long run | 200–250 | Upper |
| 4 | **Build & durability** | What the shoe looks like after N km (photo of the outsole), grip in the wet, build quality. Says plainly that N km is not a durability test | 100–150 | scattered sentences |
| 5 | **Value, and what else to look at** | Price against two or three named rivals, one paragraph each, ending "choose the X if…", each linked to its comparison; upgrade from last year or not | 300–350 | Conclusions |

- **Each scored section has three things the tester delivers:** the score, a **one-line finding** ("The best Pegasus upper yet; narrow toe box, go half a size up") and the text. The finding is what Ruggero described: "the rating breakdown will have a summary of these, then the review text will expand on those." It is also the heading hook the 21 Nov reading asked for (proposal 4).
- **No "Introduction" and no "Conclusions".** Brand history loses a quarter of the readers who start it (38 % → 28 %); the conclusion is the verdict at the top. A tester who wants the history gets two sentences in "How I tested it".
- **Impressions vs stats (idea 2):** weight, drop, stack and the ride sliders stay in the facts; the text does not repeat them. The text is what happened on the runs. The rule for the tester: every paragraph has a run in it (a distance, a pace, a day), not a specification.
- **Trail:** the same four, plus **Protection** as a described section (it has no score today). The trail text already has these sections.
- **Cost:** it is new fields and a new tester brief, not a redesign. The risk is the same as in proposal 2 (order): the personal material moves up, the history goes. **Goal 1** (answer without going back to Google) and **goal 3** (rival paragraphs link to comparisons).

## B. Scoring (question 2)
| Option | Fixes | Costs (367 reviews, comparisons, schema) | Old and new side by side? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Keep the ten | Nothing | Nothing. The new tester will again give four identical ride scores (158 of 303 have landing = transition = toe-off) | yes |
| **2. Four parts; old reviews shown in four, worked out from their ten** (recommended) | One ride score instead of four copies; the breakdown is 4 rows, not 10 bars; each part is a text section | Template: breakdown block, the "wins" rows of the compare table, schema (4 instead of 10 properties). **No review is edited**; the ten stay stored. Old parts will show halves (Pegasus 42: ride 6, fit & comfort 8.5, build & durability 8.5, value 6) | **yes** |
| 3. New scheme for new reviews only, old pages keep ten bars | Same for new reviews | Two templates for ever, and the most wanted comparison (new model vs last year's, 207 of 303 road reviews link a predecessor) is new vs old, so the mapping of option 2 is needed anyway | no |
| 4. Scores per use (daily, tempo, long run, race), as RunRepeat and RTINGS | "Best for more than one thing" | Cannot be worked out for old reviews: history break. Needs every shoe tested in every kind of run | no |

**The four parts:** Ride = sole unit, landing, transition, toe-off. Fit & comfort = comfort, upper. Build & durability = build quality, durability, traction (on trail, traction stays its own part, **Grip**). Value = value/price.
**Scored vs described:** scored = the four. Described = fit verdict, cushioning type and amount, stability, flexibility (as today), plus protection on trail.
**The expert score** stays the tester's own whole number out of 10 (listings, compare tables and schema keep working). It is **not** calculated from the parts.
**For the tester's brief, to stop the 8–10 habit:** word anchors (5 = an average shoe at its price today, 7 = good, 9 = one of the best I ran in this year, 10 = almost never), and one rule: a part's score cannot contradict its one-line finding (the Triumph 23 case: 10/10 for transition and toe-off, text says no energy return).
**What it does not fix:** differences between testers stay in the old scores. With one tester from now on, the new ones will be consistent with each other.
**A purer version, not recommended:** score only ride, fit & comfort and value, and describe build and durability (they explain nothing of the verdict). It removes three scores from 367 pages and three rows from comparisons for a small gain.

## Score and breakdown together (question 3)
Not "X vs Y", not "vs average". What a reader can take in within seconds:

```
7/10   Why 7? "A great upper on a flat ride: the ride is what you buy a Pegasus for."

Ride                 6    Dense and flat; easy runs only, 21 km is the limit        →
Fit & comfort        8.5  Best Pegasus upper yet; narrow toe box, half a size up    →
Build & durability   8.5  Tough outsole, built to last                              →
Value                6    Heavier and duller than the Cumulus 28 and Ride 19      →
```
(example lines written by me from the Pegasus 42 review, to show the shape; the arrows jump to the section.)

- The reader sees at once which part pulled the score down, which is what "the parts say X, we say Y" was trying to say, without a second number to explain.
- **If Ruggero still wants the calculated number:** show it only inside "Why 7?" and only when it is 1 point or more away ("the parts average 8.8; I give 7 because…"). On the old reviews that would be 47 of 303 with nobody to write the sentence, so it can only apply to new reviews.
- **If he still wants a number comparison:** the previous version, per part ("Ride 6, Pegasus 41: 6"), and only when the same tester scored both. Not a category average.

## Where it goes (question 4)
- **"Why 7?"** at the top, with the verdict (86 % of phone readers reach it).
- **The four rows where "Rating breakdown" is today** (73 % reach it). Not higher: above it is the phone price block, where the shop clicks happen (2.6 % of views); pushing that down costs goal 2. Four rows are about a third of a phone screen against a full screen for ten bars, so "who should buy" (41 % today) comes up by itself.
- **Not behind a tap on the badge.** Taps there are not tracked and Ruggero doubts anyone taps; the block does not need a jump to be found at 73 %.
- Each row jumps to its section; `jump_click` already counts that.

## Categories (question 5)
- **Not needed for this format**, because "versus the category average" is not recommended.
- "Best for" cannot serve as a category: 231 of 303 are "daily training" and 116 have two or more tags. The shoe type shown in compare tables has "road" on 48 shoes and "stability" on none (still to check in WordPress, as the brief says; I could not check it from here).
- **If a single type is wanted later** (for lists, similar shoes, compare suggestions): one new field, "Main use", one choice, next to "Best for" (which becomes "also good for"). It is a field, not the taxonomy behind `/reviews/all/<type>/`, so no URL, listing or old page changes. 183 road reviews have a single tag and fill themselves; 120 need a pick by hand. Its own decision, after the tester.

## Method
CSV read with pandas on 22 Nov (scripts throwaway, not kept). Four parts = plain averages of the sub-scores listed. "Explain the expert score" = share of variation in a straight-line fit (R² 0.71 for ten sub-scores, 0.71 for the four parts). Predecessor match: "Previous model" name against review titles, 126 of the 207 linked ones matched by name. Live pages: RSG Pegasus 42 and Cascadia 19; RunRepeat, RTINGS, Believe in the Run and Running Warehouse on the Pegasus 42; Road Trail Run on the Pegasus Plus 2; Solereview on the Pegasus 41.
