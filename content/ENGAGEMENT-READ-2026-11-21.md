# Reviews and comparisons: where readers stop, and what to change (21 Nov 2026)

_Ruggero's question (21 Nov): reviews and comparisons are long and people lose interest; is the order right, where do they get bored, would "jumps" help? Nothing was changed on the site. **Next chat: continue at "Proposals", Ruggero picks.**_

## What was done
- **Read in full, as a reader:** 12 reviews (Pegasus 42, Vomero Plus, Nimbus 28, Evo SL, Novablast 5 (layout only), Rebel v5, Cumulus 28, Zoom Fly 6, Kayano 32, Adrenaline GTS 25, old template: Free 5.0 (2013), Speedgoat 5) and 11 comparisons (Nimbus 28 vs Bondi 9, Vomero Premium vs Plus, Nimbus 28 vs 27, Megablast vs Superblast 2, Ghost vs Adrenaline, Nimbus vs Cumulus, Glycerin 23 vs Nimbus 28, Clifton vs Bondi, Nimbus vs Kayano, Pegasus vs Vomero, Winflo vs Pegasus): the most viewed pages of 16–21 Nov.
- **Measured each page's layout** on a phone (390 px) and desktop (1366 px) with Raptive's ad slots in place (no auctions, tracker blocked): where every section starts, words, photos, links.
- **Umami, humans only, 17–21 Nov:** price spots seen and clicked on 3,078 phone + 2,854 desktop review views and 1,561 + 1,148 comparison views (full sample); scroll depth and taps from the heatmap sample (10 % of sessions, never EU/UK): 350 review visits, 174 comparison visits, 174 + 81 taps.
- Scripts and raw exports: Mac `content/_work/engagement-2026-11-21/` (not in git).

**Limits:** 5 days of data on a design that changed during those days, in a core-update week. The heatmap sample overstates depth a little (the price block on phones: 76 % in the sample, 67 % in the full count). Section positions come from 10 reviews / 11 comparisons and are applied to all sampled visits. Good enough to see where the big drops are; not for 2–3 point differences.

## Reviews
A review on a phone is **30 screens** (22,800 px) for about 1,500 words of text, 7 photos and 11 ad slots; desktop 22 screens.

| Section (phone order) | Starts at screen | Phone readers reaching it | Desktop |

| Verdict | 2 | 86 % | 74 % |
| Pros / cons | 3 | 79 % | 66 % |
| Phone price block | 3.3 | 76 % (full count 67 %) | sidebar price card 79 % |
| Rating breakdown | 4 | 73 % | 61 % |
| The facts | 4.5 | 60 % | 47 % |
| Our tester's take | 5 | 51 % | 47 % |
| Who should / should not buy | 6.7 | 41 % | 42 % |
| Introduction | 7.4 | 38 % | 34 % |
| First impressions | 10 | 28 % | 25 % |
| Upper | 12 | 25 % | 21 % |

| Conclusions | 18 | 19 % | 17 % |
| "How does it compare" table | 21 | 14 % (full count 11.5 %) | 13 % (14.2 %) |

| Similar shoes | 27 | 1 % (2.2 %) | 2 % (2.4 %) |

1. **The top works.** One-line summary, score, verdict, pros/cons and price are seen by two thirds to three quarters, and a phone reader is through them in a few seconds (median 4 s to the price block). Shop clicks happen here: phone price block 2.6 % of views, desktop sidebar card 2.7 %; every lower spot together 1 %.
2. **A third of the readers leave in the data zone** (rating breakdown → facts → tester's take: 73 % → 41 % on phones, three screens of bars, dials and sliders). "Who should buy / should not buy", the four most useful sentences on the page, sits below it at screen 6.7.
3. **The text starts with its weakest part.** "Introduction" is brand history and market context (Pegasus in 1983, New Balance's founder and his chickens in 1906, the Evo SL hype, "Cardio Cowboy" in the Kayano review): about 330 words and 9 % of the page before anything about how this shoe runs. It loses a quarter of the readers who start it (38 % → 28 %), the steepest drop in the text. After it the line is nearly flat: who gets past the introduction mostly reaches the end.
4. **The best material is where 1 in 5 gets:** the sole unit section (how it rides, which paces, the longest run, the named rivals) at screen 14 and the conclusions (versus last year's model, versus rivals, is it worth the price) at screen 18.
5. **Readers scan, they don't read.** Phone readers who reach the conclusions get there a median 87 s after opening the page (introduction at 23 s); reading the words in between takes 5–6 minutes. The headings give a scanner nothing to stop on: "NIKE PEGASUS 42 UPPER", "NIKE PEGASUS 42 SOLE UNIT" (the shoe's name five times, no finding). The bold sentences inside the text are the only hooks, and they are good.
6. **The ways onward are at the bottom, and the best one is missing.** The comparison table is seen on 11.5 % (phone) / 14.2 % (desktop) of review views, Similar shoes on 2 %. **None of the 10 reviews links to any comparison in the page body**, although the Nimbus 28 alone has at least five (vs Nimbus 27, Bondi 9, Glycerin 23, Cumulus, Kayano), comparisons are the top landing pages and convert 2.3× reviews (GA4). Of the readers who do open a second page from a review, 2–3 % go to a comparison (about half to another review, 22 % to a listing). 75 % of phone review views and 59 % of desktop ones are the last page of the visit.
7. **Photos are the most used thing on the page:** 22 % of phone review views swipe the gallery; 42 of 74 recorded phone taps are in the top zone. The photos inside the text are beauty shots without information.
8. **Writing:** Brandon's reviews are specific (paces, distances, wear after N km, named rivals). The Kayano 32 and Adrenaline GTS 25 reviews (other tester) are thinner: "who should buy" is one generic sentence, the Kayano's "should not buy" is empty, and the text leans on phrases ("jack-of-all-runs", "New York Philharmonic") instead of findings.

## Comparisons
17 screens on a phone (12,600 px), about 770 words; desktop 12 screens.

| Section | Starts at screen (phone) | Phone | Desktop |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro text | 2 | 97 % | 82 % |
| Jump links line | 2.6 | 94 % | 81 % |
| Snapshot table (verdict box, prices, specs) | 2.8 | 91 % | 79 % |
| Score breakdown | 5 | 73 % | 47 % |
| Sole unit text | 6 | 65 % | 42 % |
| Upper text | 8.4 | 37 % | 33 % |
| Which one to buy | 10 | 33 % | 27 % |
| Best price boxes | 11 | 25 % (full count 24.5 %) | 23 % (24.3 %) |
| Latest comparisons | 14 | 12 % | 14 % |

1. **The top works here too:** the table with prices is seen on 75–79 % of all views and takes most shop clicks (deal row 2.9–3.3 % of views).
2. **Readers leave inside the sole unit text** (phone 65 % → 37 %): seven paragraphs that describe shoe A, then shoe B, feature by feature.
3. **The answer is at screen 10.** "Which one to buy" is reached by a third. On the refreshed comparisons the same answer ("Our verdict", "Buy if") is in the table, seen by about 9 in 10. Pages without the box (about 130) keep the answer where two thirds never get, and their intro ends with "This comparison will tell you which of these trainers you should buy".
4. **The jump line is not used:** 0 of 81 recorded taps on comparisons (174 sampled visits) hit "In this comparison: Snapshot · Scores · Sole unit · Upper · Which to buy · Prices". Taps go to the table (25) and to the text (25).
5. **Some comparisons contradict RSG's own reviews** (found by reading, all high-traffic pages):
   - Nimbus 28: the review says firmer than average, "even the Novablast 5 is softer". Nimbus 28 vs Bondi 9, Glycerin 23 vs Nimbus 28 and Nimbus vs Kayano say "extremely plush", "your feet sink down into the foam", "super soft".
   - Nimbus 28 vs Bondi 9: text "the Nimbus and the Bondi run true to size", its table: Bondi "buy half size bigger".
   - Nimbus 28 vs Nimbus 27: the score box gives the 27 the win on sole unit, upper, transition and toe-off; the text calls the 28 softer, lighter, with the more comfortable upper.
6. **Onward:** 86 % of phone and 79 % of desktop comparison views are the last page. Of those who continue, 53–64 % open another comparison, yet the only block for that is "Latest comparisons" (the six newest, not related ones) at screen 14.

## Jumps: what the data says
A table of contents made of section names does not get used (comparisons: 0 taps). People tap what they are looking at: the gallery, the table, the ratings and facts. So jumps should hang on those, not on a separate strip:
- a rating line ("Sole unit 6.0"), a fit result ("Toebox: tight", "Runs small"), a con ("Very narrow toe box") or a table row ("Sizing", "wins: Sole unit") jumps to the paragraph that explains it;
- where the reader decides to stay or go (after "Who should buy" on reviews, after the table on comparisons): three or four questions in the reader's words that land on the section ("How does it ride?", "Is it better than the Pegasus 41?", "Does it fit true to size?", "What else should I look at?").

## Proposals (in the order I would do them; each on staging first, one at a time, measured)
0. **Measure first:** a `section_seen` event per section on reviews and comparisons for every visitor (as `home_section_seen` on the homepage) and a `jump_click` event. One week of baseline, then every change below has a before/after on the full count, not on a 10 % sample.
1. **Comparisons: the answer at the top.** Verdict / "Buy if" box on the ~130 pages without it (already decided 18 Nov; this is the evidence), "Which one to buy" directly under the table, sole and upper text after it as the detail. Template order only; the box needs text per page.
2. **Reviews: link the comparisons.** A row "Compare it with" (the shoe's existing comparisons, predecessor first) right after "Who should / should not buy", and a link in the text where the tester names a rival that has a comparison. This is the parked "next-step block" (work list item 1 in `CONTENT-REVIEW-2026-11-20.md`); the new fact for it: anything below the conclusions is seen by fewer than 15 %.
3. **Reviews: order.** "Who should / should not buy" up under pros/cons; rating breakdown and tester's take after it. In the text: sole unit (ride) first, then upper and fit, then conclusions; the introduction cut to its two or three sentences about this model (weight, price, what changed) and the history moved to the end or dropped. The sections are separate fields, so the template can reorder without touching the text.
4. **A finding under every heading** ("Upper: the best Pegasus upper yet; very narrow, go half a size up") and the jumps above from ratings, fit and cons. Who writes the lines (tester or generated from the section, then read by a person) is open.
5. **Fix the contradictions** in the Nimbus 28 comparisons (vs Bondi 9, vs Glycerin 23, vs Kayano, vs Nimbus 27): move them up in the refresh queue (SEO checklist 2.1).
6. **Comparisons: related, not latest:** "More comparisons with the <shoe A> / <shoe B>" under "Which one to buy".
7. Later: photos in the text with a caption that says something (wear after 94 km, the toe box); the two thin reviews (Kayano 32, Adrenaline GTS 25) get a real "who should / should not buy".

**What I would not do:** shorten the reviews across the board (1,500 words is not long; the page is long because of layout and the order), or add a table of contents.
